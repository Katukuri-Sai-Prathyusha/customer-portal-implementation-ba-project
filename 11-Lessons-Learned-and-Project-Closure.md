# 11 - Lessons Learned & Project Closure

## Project

Customer Portal Implementation & Digital Transformation

# Part 1 - Lessons Learned

## 1. Purpose

Lessons learned are captured to identify what worked well, what could be improved, and what should be done differently in future projects.

The goal is to improve project delivery, stakeholder collaboration, requirement quality, and customer outcomes.

---

## 2. What Went Well

### Requirement Workshops

Structured requirement workshops helped identify business needs and clarify expectations across business and technical teams.

### Stakeholder Collaboration

Early stakeholder identification helped establish clear ownership and communication channels.

### Scope Definition

Clearly defining in-scope and out-of-scope functionality helped reduce uncontrolled scope expansion.

### RAID Management

Regular review of risks, issues, assumptions, and dependencies helped the project team identify potential delivery problems early.

### UAT Preparation

Preparing UAT scenarios based on approved requirements helped business users validate the expected customer workflows.

### Change Control

Using a formal change request process helped the team evaluate the impact of new requirements before accepting them.

---

# 3. What Could Be Improved

| Area | Observation | Improvement |
|---|---|---|
| Requirements | Some requirements required multiple clarification sessions | Use detailed acceptance criteria earlier |
| Stakeholders | Business users had limited availability | Confirm stakeholder availability during project initiation |
| Integration | Integration dependencies required additional coordination | Identify technical dependencies during discovery |
| UAT | Business users required additional preparation | Conduct UAT walkthroughs before formal testing |
| Customer Adoption | Adoption requires continuous communication | Start customer communication earlier |
| Knowledge Base | Some content required updates | Perform content review before development completion |

---

# 4. Key Lessons Learned

### Lesson 1 - Engage Stakeholders Early

Early stakeholder involvement reduces requirement ambiguity and improves decision-making.

### Lesson 2 - Define Scope Clearly

A clear scope baseline helps prevent scope creep and makes change management easier.

### Lesson 3 - Identify Dependencies Early

Technical and business dependencies should be identified during project planning rather than during execution.

### Lesson 4 - Make Requirements Testable

Acceptance criteria should be specific and measurable so that QA and business users can validate the solution effectively.

### Lesson 5 - Prepare UAT Users

Business users should understand the UAT process, test scenarios, and expected outcomes before formal UAT begins.

### Lesson 6 - Plan Change Management

Change requests should be assessed for business, technical, timeline, and resource impacts before approval.

## 7. Project Handover

After go-live, ownership will transition from the project team to the operational support teams.

### Handover Activities

- Provide system documentation.
- Provide support procedures.
- Transfer known issues.
- Confirm escalation contacts.
- Share monitoring procedures.
- Provide training materials.
- Transfer ownership of operational activities.

---

## 8. Post-Implementation Review

A post-implementation review should be conducted after the solution has been live for an agreed period.

The review should evaluate:

- Customer adoption
- Portal usage
- Self-service rate
- Support request reduction
- Customer satisfaction
- System performance
- Production incidents
- Business benefits
- Outstanding improvements
